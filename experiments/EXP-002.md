## Decision: Nasdaq-only, and the partial NYSE chunk is discarded

The NYSE pull covered 2018 alone. Including it would place NYSE names in
the universe for the first year and not afterward — a universe whose
*definition changes over time*. Every cross-sectional rank, and the
`beats B4 in N of M years` gate, would then compare years built from
different populations.

A narrower but constant universe is strictly better than a wider
time-varying one. Nasdaq-only for all 4.6 years.

## Consequence for LABEL_SPEC

LABEL_SPEC §Universe specifies NYSE, NASDAQ and AMEX. This dataset is
Nasdaq only. That is a real narrowing and must be recorded in
`docs/RESULT_TOP10.md` alongside any result derived from it. Nasdaq
carries the majority of the low-priced, high-volatility names that
dominate top-gainer lists, so the research remains meaningful — but the
claim is about Nasdaq-listed movers, not all US movers.

Adding NYSE + AMEX for the same window costs $18.69 whenever wanted.

## Unplanned benefit

Single-venue data removes the cross-venue volume problem entirely.
Summing partial venue feeds always understated consolidated volume and
biased the $1M ADV filter. Nasdaq volume for a Nasdaq-listed name is a
far larger share of its consolidated volume than any three-venue sum,
so the liquidity filter is *less* distorted than the original plan.
